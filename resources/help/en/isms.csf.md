The view shows, per scope, how far your ISMS covers the **six functions of the
NIST Cybersecurity Framework 2.0**: Govern, Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond
and Recover.

**Where the figures come from:** WorkDiary calculates in two ways. The
"Source" column names the one used:

- **NIST SoA** – the NIST CSF catalogue has been imported into the scope and
  you have recorded statements for it. The coverage comes directly from those
  statements.
- **from ISO (crosswalk)** – the catalogue has not been imported. The coverage
  is derived from your ISO/IEC 27001 SoA: each CSF category is mapped to the
  ISO references with the corresponding content.

Without the NIST catalogue, a notice points out the derivation. For a direct
assessment, load the catalogue under "Requirements & SoA".

**Reading the coverage:** Only applicable requirements are counted. Anything
implemented or partially implemented counts as covered; the ratio of covered
to applicable is shown in brackets. The overall coverage combines all six
functions. "No data" means that neither NIST statements nor mapped ISO
statements exist for the function.

**CSF → ISO crosswalk:** The second page discloses the mapping: for each CSF
category the ISO/IEC 27001:2022 references and their coverage from the ISO
SoA. This shows which ISO statements are behind a weak CSF category. The
version and date of the crosswalk are shown at the top.

**Scope:** If there are several scopes, select one with the filter. Without a
selection the default scope applies.

> **Important:** The evaluation is a professional self-assessment and not a
> statement of conformity. Coverage derived through the crosswalk is an
> approximation – it does not replace your own assessment against the NIST
> catalogue.
