A **lead** is a prospect **before** customer status — from a referral, the
web, a trade fair or a call. The lead file holds contact, source and need;
qualification runs through the **communication notes** with follow-up, just
like on a customer.

**Leads from appointment bookings.** When someone without a customer record
books through Calendly, a lead with the source “Appointment booking” can be
created. Each organization turns this on in the Calendly settings; it is off by
default. If an existing customer could match, no lead is created — the booking
then waits in the assignment inbox. A repeat booking from the same address
attaches to the open lead.

## The pipeline

**New → Contacted → Qualified → Converted / Discarded.** A discarded lead can
be requalified; a converted one lives on as a customer.

## Converting — with duplicate check

Before creating, the file shows **possible existing customers** (name,
email). Linking is the equal option — a second customer master through the
back door is exactly what duplicate clean-up laboriously repairs later. The
lead file remains after conversion and points to the customer: the
acquisition history belongs to the acquisition.

## Retention

Leads are personal data **without a contract**. Non-converted leads are
anonymised **6 months after the last contact** — via the retention scan of
the privacy module (review queue, no silent deletion). The pipeline metric
(source, status, period) stays, the person disappears.

## Record

Leads carry custom fields (Administration › Custom fields), tags and
attachments. When the lead is anonymised after the deletion period,
attachments, custom fields and tags are removed as well. Documents in document
management are attached to the customer only.

## Cockpit

The lead record is the prospect's cockpit: header with status, source and
tags, below that contact and needs, the communication notes with follow-up,
custom fields, attachments, the quotes created from the lead and the history.
"Quote from lead" creates a quote with the contact details; the quotes are
listed with their status in the record, and the lead report counts them per
source and conversion. As for customer, supplier and project, the panels of
other modules only show what exists for the lead — a module without data for
the lead does not appear. The history lists the audit entries of the lead
including its quotes; appointment bookings are attached to the open lead.
