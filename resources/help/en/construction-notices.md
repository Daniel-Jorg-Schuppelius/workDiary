Two formal letters to the client that German VOB/B construction law
recognises:

- The **notice of hindrance** (§ 6 (1) VOB/B) reports that execution is
  obstructed — by missing preliminary work, weather, missing approvals. It is
  the precondition for an extension of the completion deadline.
- The **notice of concern** (§ 4 (3) VOB/B) raises concerns about the
  intended method of execution, about preliminary work by others, or about
  supplied materials. It is the way to limit one’s own liability.

## Why timing matters

Both letters take effect **on receipt**, not retroactively. A notice of
hindrance written two weeks after the hindrance began does not cover those
two weeks. So: write it immediately, even if not all consequences are known
yet.

## What the letter carries over

Customer, project, location and — where recorded — the **weather snapshot**
are taken from the diary entry. Especially for weather-related hindrances,
documented conditions at the time of the event are the difference between an
assertion and evidence.

## The printout is frozen

A sent letter is rendered with the **design state at the time of sending**. A
later change of company stationery therefore does not alter the proof of
receipt after the fact — otherwise a document would be in play that was never
sent in that form.
