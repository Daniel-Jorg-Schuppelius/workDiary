**Tender documents arrive as a package**, not as a single file: a ZIP holding
the bill of quantities, terms of participation, drawings and forms. The package
intake splits it into two kinds of content:

- **GAEB files stay proposals.** They are detected, stored and listed — the
  import happens on request. A package may hold several lots of which only one
  is relevant; importing a bill of quantities blindly would mean accepting it
  unchecked.
- **Everything else goes into document management** and attaches to the tender
  case. Without an assigned case only GAEB files are taken over: remaining
  documents would have no case to belong to and would be unfindable.

**The format family comes from the content, not the extension.** Portals name
the same file differently depending on the tool; detection reads the first
bytes. The same file never appears twice — portals republish documents with
every correction.

**Bid submission runs through the assistant** in three steps: check, export,
document. The check comes deliberately *before* submission, because remedying a
bid is the exception in procurement law — an incomplete bid is excluded. The
assistant distinguishes:

- **Blockers** stop the submission: no go decision, an already decided case,
  open mandatory documents.
- **Warnings** stop no one: missing procedure type, missing binding period,
  missing bid value, unpriced items — and the **expired submission deadline**.
  The submission is *documented* here, often on the following day; being unable
  to record an already submitted bid does not make the case more complete, it
  makes it wrong.

Among the items the assistant counts only those **without a unit price** that
are **not marked “not offered”** — exactly this distinction separates a
deliberate non-offer from a gap.

**The opening result** records what was read out at the opening session or what
the information letter names. Your own bid is one of the rows — only then is the
price gap readable. The rank is recorded, not calculated: it may differ from the
price order.
